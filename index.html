01
THE PHYSICS

A battery is a quantum state

Each cell has ground state |0⟩ and excited state |1⟩, with energy gap ω. The stored energy is the expectation of Hᵦ; the dynamics depend on the drive and interactions.

Read the model

Hᵦ = ω Σᵢ |1⟩ᵢ⟨1|. The total model adds (Ω/√N) Σᵢ Xᵢ and (g/√M) Σ over edges of (σ⁺ᵢσ⁻ⱼ + σ⁻ᵢσ⁺ⱼ). A star also has a hub energy ω and starts that hub in |1⟩; local drive and edge coefficients are normalized by their L2 budgets.

02
USEFUL ENERGY

Stored energy isn't the whole story

Ergotropy measures the maximum work extractable by a unitary transformation from the current battery state. A mixed state can hold energy that is not fully extractable as work.

How we estimate it

For the star model, the battery is reduced from the battery-plus-hub pure state and its eigenvalues are rearranged into a passive state. For the other topologies, the battery stays pure in this model, so its ergotropy equals stored energy. This is computed from the simulated state, not inferred from measurement counts alone.

03
FAIR COMPARISON

Define the resource before claiming advantage

Different graph structures can change the charging curve, but comparing them fairly requires careful accounting of drive strength, coupling weights, charger resources and the time window.

What this demo does not claim

The star starts with an excited auxiliary hub, so it begins with extra energy. The plots report outcomes of the stated toy model, not a resource-independent quantum advantage, a calibrated device prediction, or a practical battery design.
